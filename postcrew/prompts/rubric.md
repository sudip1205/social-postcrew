HOW EVERY POST IS SCORED (the Writer writes to this guide; the Editor scores by it; each criterion 0-100):

hook - the first line only, as shown above "...see more"
  90+   at most 120 characters; leads with ONE concrete sourced specific (a number, a named product or company,
        a date, a command) AND makes the reader's stake obvious (what changes for them). Plain words.
  75-89 concrete and sourced, but 120-150 characters, or the reader's stake is only implied.
  60-74 a summary or topic label ("X and Y are developing a standard for..."), several facts crammed into one
        line, or so hedged it loses its force.
  <60   generic, a question, clickbait, or not supported by the sources.
  Qualifiers ("plans to", "says") belong in the hook only when the fact needs them; put the rest of the
  context in line two.

value
  90+   the reader can apply it this week: 2-3 specific steps (who to ask, what to check, what to try, the
        command or code to run) using what exists now - plus why it is worth their time.
  70-89 useful but generic ("review your APIs"), or only one concrete step.
  <70   nothing to do except "watch this space" / "read it when it is out".

clarity
  90+   one read is enough for a mid-level professional; one idea per paragraph; a term the audience may not
        know is either dropped or explained in a few plain words that are common knowledge.
  70-89 one confusing passage, or jargon that a reader has to guess.
  <70   a list of terms, out of order, or needs re-reading.

accuracy
  90+   every factual or technical sentence is backed by the sources, code or reference facts, with their
        tense and qualifiers ("plans to", "in preview", "up to"); numbers match the sources exactly; code is
        correct; opinions are labelled as opinions.
  70-89 one claim slightly stronger or vaguer than its source.
  <70   any claim the sources do not support (also a red flag: unsupported_claim / factual_error).

tone    plain, professional, no hype words or sales pitch, no engagement bait. 90+ reads like a practitioner
        talking to peers.
format  the category's length, short paragraphs, plain text (no markdown), one question then 3-4 hashtags on the
        last line, no source list or links in the body (the primary link goes in the first comment), and the
        image text repeats the post's own wording.

A post that would score 85+ on every criterion is short and focused: one story, one concrete hook, the facts the
reader needs, 2-3 practical steps, one question. Every extra paragraph, definition or caveat is a chance to lose
points on clarity and accuracy - cut rather than add.
